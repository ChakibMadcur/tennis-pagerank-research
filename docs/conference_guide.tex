{\Large\bfseries Where to present and publish the WPR work}\\[0.3em]
Chakib Madcur \quad·\quad checked 27 October 2026 \quad·\quad deadlines marked ``not posted'' must be confirmed on the official site

\textbf{What the work is, for choosing venues.} A network-science method (weighted PageRank on directed graphs of results) applied to sports rankings, with a fifty-year tennis study, an upset experiment and a cross-sport comparison. That fits three communities: \textbf{sports analytics and statistics}, \textbf{network science}, and \textbf{computer science / data mining}.

\textbf{Three kinds of venue.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Abstract conferences (talk or poster).} You send a 1--2 page abstract. Nothing is formally published, but it goes on the CV as a presentation. Fast and low risk; good for a first conference.
\item \textbf{Conferences with published proceedings.} You send a full or short paper (8--12 pages). If it is accepted it is \emph{published} (e.g., by Springer), which counts as a publication on the CV.
\item \textbf{Journals.} The strongest publication. Review takes months, so it is the step after the semester presentation.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Journals (after the semester presentation)}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Journal of Sports Analytics} (IOS Press / Sage): open access with \textbf{no publication fee} (DOAJ record).
\item \textbf{Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports} (American Statistical Association, De Gruyter): ``team ranking methods'' is listed in its scope; double-anonymous review.
\item \textbf{International Journal of Computer Science in Sport} (Sciendo): open access since 2016; check the fee page before submitting.
\item \textbf{Applied Network Science} (Springer Open): network-science applications; open access with an article fee.
\end{itemize}
